3. DOCUMENT THE CUSTOMER WORKFLOW

1. Customer selects the package, room, session, guests, and add-ons.
2. Backend supplies a versioned quote and checks booking eligibility.
3. Checkout offers Online Payment and Bank Deposit / Transfer.
4. For bank payment, create a linked payment request and provisional hold.
5. Display verified business bank details, exact payable amount, currency,
   unique request/reference, deadlines, review hours, and hold status.
6. Customer deposits cash at a bank or transfers funds to that account.
7. Customer uploads evidence and submits payment details.
8. System acknowledges submission and notifies authorized reviewers.
9. Customer sees Payment Under Review, not Paid or Confirmed.
10. Reviewer matches funds against the business's own bank records.
11. Successful verification records the payment and its allocation.
12. If the reservation and required eligibility checks remain valid,
    confirm it and queue notifications and smart-access provisioning.

Recommended initial scope:
- Full quoted payment before confirmation, not installment booking.
- Each payment request relates to a specific package, room, date, and time.
- No wallet, transferable credit, or open-date voucher is implied.
- Third-party payers can be supported with adequate matching information;
  a different payer name alone need not mean fraud.

Receipt submission fields:
Booking/payment-request reference, receipt file, declared amount,
payment date/time, bank reference where available, optional payer name,
and explanatory note. Distinguish customer-declared fields from verified
bank-side facts. Preserve earlier submissions when requesting corrections.

Explain that receipts acknowledge a payment claim, not bank confirmation.
Do not call the uploaded document a CineNest-issued payment receipt.

Never request banking passwords, PINs, OTPs, or card security codes.

7. COVER MONEY AND SERVICE EXCEPTIONS

Document expected handling for:

- Underpayment: record received amount and outstanding balance; do not
  confirm as fully paid. Full-payment launch scope does not erase partial
  money that actually arrived.
- Overpayment: allocate only what is due; retain the excess for controlled
  refund/resolution. Do not count it as extra booking revenue.
- Same transaction claimed against multiple bookings: prevent duplicate
  allocation. Shared-deposit/multi-booking allocations are future scope
  unless separately approved.
- Customer pays through both bank and gateway: reconcile both attempts,
  confirm only one reservation, and resolve duplicate funds.
- Receipt rejected before funds later arrive: reopen reconciliation.
- Wrong account, wrong currency, missing reference, unreadable evidence,
  unmatched bank credit, or suspicious submission: explicit review states.
- Expired/cancelled booking with received funds: retain the financial record
  and offer an approved resolution without silently creating a new booking.
- Reviewer notification failure: submission remains visible and escalates.
- Access provisioning failure: payment remains verified; retry or open an
  access incident rather than pretending payment failed.
- Refund: distinguish requested, approved, initiated, sent, confirmed,
  and failed as supported by evidence. Verify the destination and record
  the outgoing transfer; approval alone is not a completed refund.

Do not assume a card token exists for bank-paying customers.
Automatic damage card re-billing is not available merely because a bank
booking was approved. Any alternative requires a separate approved policy.

The existing LKR 100 convenience fee needs an explicit bank-method decision.
Do not assume it is waived or retained. Show all approved fees before
payment and preserve the quoted treatment. Do not invent taxes or bank fees.
